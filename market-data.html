How often health insurance says no

Denial rates, appeal outcomes, and prior authorization denials from KFF analyses of federal data and HHS Inspector General reports.

The denial that makes the news is usually a single patient's fight. The data behind it is quieter and larger: one in five claims on the federal marketplace was denied in 2023, the highest in-network rate since tracking began, while fewer than one in a hundred denied claims ever gets appealed. In Medicare Advantage post-acute care, the pattern sharpens further — denials that collapse on appeal at a 95% rate, suggesting the first decision, not the patient's case, was the problem.

How to read these numbers

Included: KFF's analyses of CMS transparency filings for HealthCare.gov plans (2020 and 2023) and the HHS Office of Inspector General's June 2026 Medicare Advantage prior authorization reports. Excluded: employer-plan denial rates, which are not publicly reported in comparable form, and any estimate of total national denials that blends incompatible plan types. Where two secondary reports of the same KFF release differ on a decimal, the figure carried here is the one corroborated by at least two independent reports of the release.

Disclaimer: A denial is not always a final refusal — claims resubmitted and paid are excluded from KFF's denial rate — and marketplace-plan figures do not describe employer plans or state-based marketplaces.
